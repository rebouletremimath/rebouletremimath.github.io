IN GENERAL.

From the purely mathematical standpoint, I believe AI to be a great tool. The richer fruits I have harvested so far come from collaborating with it: by prompting about my own ideas, especially ones I have already been exploring for years. It seems profoundly helpful in bringing some ideas to life, speeding up some technical work, and tearing down barriers (such as: two communities using vastly different languages but using the same objects, making understanding the other community's articles difficult and unrewarding, for example).

It may be the case that AI becomes better at coming up with original ideas in the future, but this currently does not seem to be the case (caveat: as far as I can tell, with my limited skills in using AI tools, and the models that I have access to). I cannot claim that I will keep this collaboration-only mindset forever, as it could be the case that AI becomes able to write billions of excellent papers per second some time in the future, yet again uprooting any practices we may have gotten used to. As the evolution of that technology is essentially impossible to accurately predict, it is also an impossible task to prepare for the future; thus, I prefer to adapt and react to the current circumstances.

Our lived experience of mathematics is, ultimately, what interests me in mathematics: "living within" the objects, what images and emotions concepts evoke to me, the "technique" or "yoga" of working by hand with certain ideas... I do not believe AI usage, or the AI's existence, to be intrinsically incompatible with this experience of mathematics. Prompting and exploring ideas I genuinely like and care about, advancing my knowledge of them, is still "living mathematics"; as opposed to, for example, prompting AI about a conjecture I have never thought about, may not even understand, and asking it to give me a binary "true/false" answer in Lean. This is not to be taken antagonistically: it is fine to me that people work in this way; it does not interest me personally. Likewise, I do not think negatively of colleagues who wish to not use AI in their work.

I refuse to make any philosophical statements about the nature of mathematics with the authority of a practicing mathematician: this is far from my field of expertise. All comments above pertain to my lived experience of the job of researching mathematics. As a simple person, I quite like the view, beautifully explained by Lafforgue in this text (backup), that all things intrinsically deserve to be studied - even the most abstract and seemingly far removed from reality; and that all things hold infinite depth and beauty, and are infinitely intelligible.

One aspect of our job is to be (and train) human beings able to understand high-level mathematical proofs. This by definition cannot be changed by AI. The intricate prestige system inherent to academic mathematics, which underlies all of the more prideful aspects of our job: competition to be the smartest person, proving well-known conjectures, attaining fame and glory, will likely die. How freeing that is! Bon débarras !

IN MATHEMATICAL ARTICLES.

I believe that retaining a human component in my mathematical papers is essential. For me, this will be upheld in practice by guaranteeing that I write all of my articles "by hand", myself, without any AI assistance, correction, rephrasing tools, etc. I think the "literary" content of papers is profoundly human. I insist that this view is independent of the relative human skills to AI skills. While it is currently the case, I do not believe humans will remain better technical/matheamtical writers than AI's for long. As of writing this statement, AI-written papers are jarring to read, but this will likely soon change. I am also not claiming to be very skilled in writing.

For the moment (papers written from the date of this statement on), I will add a section in my articles stating how much AI was used. This is required by my job, but is also important in this transitional period. I do not think that this will be necessary for much longer, as AI becomes a natural tool much as paper, printers, computers, search engines, the arXiv, and (La)TeX have become throughout the years.
